# Single target model — Joint infection and survival (17 November 2026)

**Decision:** stop adding standalone SEI model variants after Stages 80, 82 and 83. Retain those as exploratory benchmarks. Develop, test and fit **one joint infection-survival model** in a single codebase.

## Scientific estimands

Estimate the separate associations of standardized multilocus homozygosity with (i) susceptible-to-exposed infection acquisition, (ii) exposed-to-infectious progression, and (iii) mortality, while allowing the current infection state to affect mortality **and repeated badger observations to have persistent individual-level frailty**. A claim of mediation or causality would require additional assumptions beyond this observational association model.

## Target joint model

- **Latent quarterly states:** S (susceptible), E (exposed), I (infectious), D (dead, absorbing). Transitions S→E, E→I, and S/E/I→D. No forced observed disease state after the last test.
- **Genetic, sex and age effects are mandatory in ALL THREE biological processes:** fit separate coefficients for homozygosity and sex in S→E acquisition, E→I progression, and mortality. Use each individual's age **at the current quarter** (not only at entry), calculated from its recorded/inferred birth quarter. The mortality age baseline is Gompertz–Makeham; acquisition and progression use an explicitly regularized, reasonably flexible age function. Standardize homozygosity once in the original genotyped cohort. Do not simply include sex/age as predictors of initial infection status.
- **Individual badger heterogeneity is mandatory:** include persistent individual-level, mean-zero, partially pooled random intercepts (frailties) for **acquisition, progression, and mortality**: `b_SE[i], b_EI[i], b_D[i]`. Use a joint hierarchical multivariate normal prior with an estimable, regularized covariance matrix (non-centred parameterization; weakly informative scale priors and a shrinkage prior on correlations). This allows the same badger to have consistently higher/lower risks than another badger of the same sex, age and homozygosity, and permits shared underlying susceptibility/vulnerability. Random effects represent **residual heterogeneity**, not genetically driven differences; avoid re-centering or absorbing the fixed homozygosity effect.
- **Individual detection heterogeneity:** model repeated-capture propensity separately from biological frailties, if the observed capture histories support its estimation. A persistent individual detection random intercept with shrinkage is the planned starting point. Do not interpret low capture as low survival, and do not silently introduce perfect detection.
- **Identifiability:** the variance and correlations of the biological random effects, especially progression frailty, may be weakly informed by observed diagnostic histories. Assess recovery/mixing of the variance components in simulations of this ONE joint model; report prior sensitivity and uncertainty transparently if some components cannot be identified. Do not automatically replace the random-effects model with a series of unrelated fixed-effects models.
- **Historical infection pressure:** one regularized, smoothly varying calendar baseline from the outset. Avoid a sequence of linear/quadratic/period scientific models. Age effects can be flexible where warranted, but one fixed primary specification must be selected before seeing new genetic results.
- **Mortality:** Gompertz–Makeham age-dependent baseline; add sex, homozygosity and current infection-state effects. Model survival jointly with disease, not by assigning each animal a fixed inferred lifetime infection status.
- **Entry:** explicitly model S/E/I probabilities at first confirmed live capture, including age, sex, calendar year and homozygosity, with left truncation. Acknowledge survivorship/genotyping selection.
- **Observation:** distinguish a known live capture, monitored but uncaptured, and not monitored (survival history codes 1/0/-1). A non-capture is not a known death. Model imperfect capture probability, observing the actual study windows and effort information. Retain repeated captures within quarters and stack usable assay results.
- **Known death recoveries:** integrate recorded death quarters with a recovery probability and censoring. Input validation on 17 November 2026 established **295 recorded deaths, 79 individuals both captured alive and recorded dead in the same quarter, zero later live records after recorded death, and zero discrepancies across 8,332 live-capture quarters**. Implement the observational sequence **live capture/tests at the beginning of a quarterly interval; disease/survival transition and any recorded death at the end**. This permits the 79 same-quarter live/dead observations without deletion or changing quarters. The original encounter source-builder holds deaths followed by later dated live encounters; date-level provenance and reason/cause of death (including intentional removals/euthanasia) remain essential checks before a final mortality interpretation.
- **Diagnostics:** six distinct assays (BELT, indirect ELISA, culture, DPP, IGRA, StatPak). Fixed original sensitivity, specificity and exposed-state multipliers; each assay's properties constant through time, while availability/test mix can change. Ambiguous outcomes stay missing; not negative. Document provisional mixed-culture rules.
- **Primary exclusions:** the 317 non-confirmed-live original-only badger-quarter occasions remain excluded; never delete the source database records.
- **Cohort:** start with the full 1,603 matched genotyped survival-input individuals, not just the 1,224 with two or more diagnostic quarters. Confirm eligibility/identity and preserve one-test/no-test animals who supply encounter or survival information. Do not impute unobserved genotypes or assume all 1,603 pass checks.

## One implementation, not many models

The implementation has begun with **one four-state forward likelihood conditional on individual frailties**, including a pure R reference and a NIMBLE compiled implementation. The same codebase must now incorporate **hierarchical inference that integrates/samples frailties**, rather than starting standalone models. It must integrate over the unobserved disease/death states and observed capture/diagnostic process. Validate transition ordering against small enumerated synthetic histories, then recover known parameters in simulations generated from **the same full joint model**. Pilot runs and alternate starts test implementation, not new biological model specifications.

Before fitting, check that genetic IDs, q indices, death times, first-capture entry and detection codes agree across the private existing inputs. Do not silently overwrite or repair records. Next fit the one model on the complete qualifying cohort, quantify parameter uncertainty (including individual random-effect standard deviations and correlations) and posterior/predictive fit to diagnostic tests, recaptures and deaths.

## Implementation status — 17 November 2026

- Cohort audit passed on rstudio04n: all 1,603 genotyped survival individuals linked exactly; 8,332 live-capture quarter keys exactly concordant with Stage76; 295 selected death quarters; 79 same-quarter live-capture/death histories, 0 impossible later-live events; 21,526 observed assay outcomes. The 379 individuals with only one diagnostic quarter are retained.
- Four-state forward likelihood passed 20 real-history checks and independent R/interpreted NIMBLE/compiled NIMBLE comparisons at 2.84e-14 absolute maximum log-likelihood difference.
- Code: **R/joint_sei_survival_forward_nimble.R**, and **R/joint_sei_survival_hierarchy_nimble.R**. The latter is ONE Bayesian model, not a new standalone SEI variant. Its individual four-dimensional noncentred latent effects give correlated biological frailties via a lower-triangular factor for S->E/E->I/death and independent capture heterogeneity. The estimable triangular loadings regularize correlations; positive diagonal scales guarantee a positive-definite covariance. Its mortality baseline is anchored GM and both infection hazards have smooth five-year random-walk calendar effects; each biological process has age, sex and homozygosity terms (GM for mortality).
- The unchanged main script **scripts/joint_sei_survival_model.R** now implements **MODE=hierarchical_compile** on the same 20 representative matched real individuals. This is an initial Bayesian-model compile and density-equivalence gate, not posterior inference. **MODE=hierarchical_benchmark** times only two trial sweeps of that same model if compilation succeeds. The full 1,603-individual Bayesian posterior will only be launched after these pass and computational feasibility is assessed.
- The diagnostic emission probabilities remain fixed per assay through time in the primary analysis, with historical variation in test availability implicitly represented by the actual test records.
- **Still pending:** execute hierarchical compilation, assess MCMC mixing/runtime, resolve informative capture monitoring proxy and whether all reported deaths can be interpreted as natural rather than removal/euthanasia, check posterior recovery on simulated histories, then full hierarchical fit and uncertainty. The present results are not the scientific posterior.
